Practical guide

AI Inbox Management for Small Business

Workers spend an average of 28 percent of their workweek reading and responding to email, according to McKinsey Global Institute research. The founding pilot is built for US small businesses — typically 1 to 20 people — that have recurring inbox work and no current case for a full-time specialist.

What it does

Triage, draft, route, follow up.

The AI Operations Employee reads and classifies every incoming message by type: inquiry, booking request, complaint, vendor message, or follow-up.

For messages within its written authority boundary, it drafts a reply and sends after your review. Messages outside that boundary are routed to you with a summary.

It tracks unresolved threads and follows up automatically when a thread goes unanswered past the agreed threshold.

Not a filter

Email rules sort. AI inbox management understands.

Email filters match keywords and move messages. They do not read a complaint and decide whether it needs an apology or a refund, and they cannot draft a reply to an appointment request using the right tone.

The AI Operations Employee routes work to the right person with a summary of what was requested and what action is needed — not just a label on a folder.

Task types

What the inbox typically contains.

For a small business with a busy inbox, recurring message types include: client inquiries, appointment requests, booking confirmations, vendor invoices and questions, complaints, follow-ups on outstanding work, and intake forms submitted through the website.

The employee tracks all of this and produces a daily or weekly plain-number report: volume by category, reply rate, and open threads.

Authority

The work scope and authority boundary are agreed in writing before the employee starts.

You define what it handles automatically and what it holds for your review. You can adjust the boundary at any time. This is not a set-it-and-forget-it chatbot — it operates inside rules you control.
