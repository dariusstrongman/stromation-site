Before day one

Your authority rules shape every decision.

The AI Operations Employee operates inside rules you write at onboarding. These define what the employee can handle automatically, what requires your approval, and what never leaves the inbox.

AUTOMATIC

  • Reply to inquiries asking about hours, location, or pricing
  • Confirm bookings that fit the shared calendar
  • File and tag every vendor invoice — send a daily digest
  • Send the weekly summary report every Monday at 8am

APPROVAL REQUIRED

  • New contacts asking about contracts or agreements
  • Complaints or negative reviews — draft reply but hold for approval
  • Requests involving refunds or credits
  • Anything that does not clearly fit the above

NEVER

  • Send money or approve payments
  • Make promises about delivery dates or scope
  • Reply to legal notices or regulatory mail

These rules are written once, saved, and survive staff changes. You can adjust them at any time from the settings panel.

What happens with five real message types

How classification drives action — automatic, draft, or archive.

Messages are classified into patterns, then actioned according to your authority rules. Here is the flow on a typical day.

inquiry

"What are your hours this weekend?"

Sender: Sarah Kim, sarah.kim@example.com

The employee extracts the question, checks your published hours in the contact details you shared, and replies automatically using your voice. No approval needed.

Action: Automatic reply using pre-approved hours template.

follow-up

"Following up on the quote you sent"

Sender: Mike Chen, mike@buildco.com

Mike has waited 3 days. The employee recognizes this as a follow-up, extracts the quote ID, and drafts a reply that references your pricing and next steps. Because the reply might commit to a timeline, it waits for your approval.

Action: Draft reply, hold for approval, flag as potential commitment.

vendor

"Calling about your insurance renewal"

Sender: Lisa at ProCare Insurance, lisa@procare.com

This is vendor outreach. The employee files it under "insurance", extracts the contact details and deadline, and adds it to the daily digest. No reply is sent because this does not require one per your rules.

Action: Tag, file, add to daily digest — no reply.

complaint

"I'm unhappy with my last appointment"

Sender: Alex Rivera, alex.r@clinic.com

A negative experience. The employee captures sentiment, extracts the appointment ID, and drafts a compassionate reply that offers to make things right. Because complaints can escalate, the reply holds for your approval and the card is flagged with priority urgency.

Action: Draft reply, hold for approval, flag priority for rapid response.

internal

Subject: "Team sync notes — Aug 26"

Sender: internal note from your team shared drive link

This is a shared document from your team. The employee extracts key action items, tags them by priority, and adds them to your daily summary without sending any reply.

Action: Extract action items, add to daily summary — no reply.
