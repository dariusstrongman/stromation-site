Small Business Operations Automation Guide

Stromation's AI Operations Employee is a founding-pilot product for small businesses. The role handles inbox triage, repeatable operations, and repeatable reporting inside a written authority boundary.

Inbox triage

Correspondence that needs attention, sorted.

A small-business inbox may contain quotes, bookings, support requests, and intake forms. A consistent triage process can keep those requests separated and easier to route.

The AI Operations Employee is designed to classify incoming work, extract useful details, assign a priority, and route work inside its written boundary.

Watch

One inbox, many sources

The role can cover incoming email, contact forms, and online intake work that falls inside the agreed scope.

Sort

Priority and routing

The role can classify and route requests according to the operating rules defined for the job.

Follow-up

Recorded follow-up

The role can record follow-up work and surface reminders when the operating scope calls for them.

Workload measurement

Measure the baseline before estimating savings.

Start with your own operating data: count the messages or requests handled each week and measure the average minutes of human work per item.

Weekly requests × average minutes per request ÷ 60 gives a baseline number of human hours. Run the same measurement after the pilot begins and compare the observed result.

This page does not promise a workload reduction or dollar savings figure. Actual results depend on message volume, task complexity, written scope, and review requirements.

Workflow model

Event-driven workflow with a written boundary.

In an event-driven workflow, software starts work when something happens, such as a new email, a form submission, or a scheduled follow-up.

For the AI Operations Employee, the job is bounded to the work defined for the role and produces an operating record of what happened.

Before work starts, the written authority boundary defines what the employee may handle automatically and what must wait for review or approval.

Authority

Scope and authority, defined before work starts.

Before work starts, you set the scope in writing: what the employee handles automatically and what it holds for your review.

Automatic inside the agreed scope

  • Reading and classifying incoming mail and forms
  • Sorting, extracting, and record-keeping
  • Drafting replies for the review queue
  • Internal routing and follow-up reminders
  • Daily or weekly reporting

Held for approval

  • Important external messages
  • Any spending
  • Account changes
  • Commitments made on your behalf
  • Anything outside the written job
Onboarding

A three-step onboarding sequence.

Three steps: agree on scope, connect the inbox, receive the first report.

  1. Fit and authority review

    Confirm the work in scope, where it routes, and what waits for approval.

  2. Connected and watching

    Connect the agreed inbox or intake source for the employee's bounded work.

  3. First report

    Use the first operating report as a starting point for reviewing outcomes and considering adjustments.

Fit

Built for a small business with a busy inbox.

The founding-pilot role targets recurring digital operations such as quotes, bookings, support requests, intake, and activity tracking.

Examples within the target work

  • B2B sales with quote responses
  • Service booking and scheduling
  • Customer support questions
  • Online intake forms
  • Daily activity tracking

Outside the founding-pilot target

  • Complex negotiation
  • Litigation strategy
  • Human resources decisions
  • Accounting journal entries
  • Marketing creative direction
