Inbox triage

Correspondence that needs attention, sorted.

A small-business inbox may contain quotes, bookings, support requests, and intake forms. A consistent triage process can keep those requests separated and easier to route.

The AI Operations Employee is designed to classify incoming work, extract useful details, assign a priority, and route work inside its written boundary.

Sort

Priority and routing

Follow-up

Deadlines without memory loss

The role can record follow-up work and surface reminders when the operating scope calls for them.

Workload calculation

An illustrative workload calculation.

Consider an example with 40 messages per week: 20 quotes, 10 support issues, and 10 bookings. For this illustration, assume each response takes five minutes to draft and ten minutes to review.

Under those assumptions, 40 messages × 15 minutes equals 10 hours per week, or 40 hours across a four-week month.

Now assume AI assistance reduces the remaining human review in this example to 4 hours per month. That shifts 36 hours away from manual handling.

Using an example labor rate of $30/hour, 36 hours equals $1,080 of time value. The AI Operations Employee is $199 per month. These figures are illustrative assumptions, not promised savings; actual results depend on message volume, task complexity, scope, and review requirements.

How AI employees work

Event-driven work with a written boundary.

In an event-driven workflow, software starts work when something happens, such as a new email, a form submission, or a scheduled follow-up.

For the AI Operations Employee, the job is bounded to the work defined for the role and produces an operating record of what happened.

Before work starts, the written authority boundary defines what the employee may handle automatically and what must wait for review or approval.

Authority

Scope and authority, defined before work starts.

Before work starts, you set the scope in writing: what the employee handles automatically and what it holds for your review.

Automatic

  • Reading and classifying incoming mail and forms
  • Sorting, extracting, and record-keeping
  • Drafting replies for your review queue
  • Internal routing and follow-up reminders
  • Daily/weekly reporting

Requires your approval

  • Important external messages
  • Any spending
  • Account changes
  • Commitments made on your behalf
  • Anything outside the written job
Onboarding

A three-step onboarding sequence.

Three steps: agree on scope, connect the inbox, receive the first report.

  1. Fit and authority review

    Confirm the work in scope, where it routes, and what waits for approval.

  2. Connected and watching

    Connect the agreed inbox or intake source for the employee's bounded work.

  3. First report

    Use the first operating report as a starting point for reviewing outcomes and considering adjustments.
